# Electrical Isolation / De-Isolation Safety Permit
## Field Extraction and Enterprise Data-Model Specification

**Source:** Electrical Isolation / De-Isolation supplementary safety permit form  
**Purpose:** Convert the existing paper form into a structured Django/CMMS/Permit-to-Work model suitable for a refinery environment.

> **Important:** The field extraction below is based on the supplied refinery form. The sections explicitly marked **Recommended** are design recommendations for a structured digital system; they are not additional fields visibly present on the paper form.

---

## 1. Design Principles

The paper form should **not** be converted into one large Django model with dozens of unrelated columns.

A professional digital implementation should separate:

1. **Permit identity and common Permit-to-Work information**
2. **Electrical isolation request**
3. **Isolation requirements and applied controls**
4. **Isolation authorization/sign-off**
5. **Isolation status / padlock records**
6. **Cable disconnection and reconnection records**
7. **Work completion / cancellation**
8. **De-isolation authorization and execution**
9. **Audit history**

This fits the existing Permit architecture, where common permit information such as permit number, permit type, workflow, current workflow step, work order, location/tag, scope of work, validity and personnel information already belongs to the main `Permit` model. The existing project also uses workflow-controlled lifecycle steps, so the electrical isolation data should complement—not duplicate—the common Permit model. fileciteturn0file0L1-L30

# 5. Isolation Control Checklist

The form uses a very useful structure:

```text
Requirement Status
    Required: Yes / No
    Applied: Yes
```

This should be preserved digitally.

## 5.1 Required Isolation Actions

### Main isolation controls

1. **Breaker Switched Off**
   - Confirm the circuit breaker has been switched to the OFF position.

2. **Racking Out / Down Breaker**
   - Confirm the breaker has been physically racked out/down where applicable.

3. **Shutters Locked**
   - Confirm shutters associated with the switchgear/breaker are secured in the required safe position.

4. **Switching Main Isolator to OFF**
   - Confirm the main isolator has been placed in the OFF position.

5. **Locked-Off C.B. / Isolator**
   - Confirm the circuit breaker or isolator has been locked in the safe position.

6. **Removal of Main Fuses**
   - Confirm main fuses have been removed where required.

7. **No Voltage Checking**
   - Confirm absence-of-voltage verification has been performed.

8. **Earthing at Cubicle**
   - Confirm the cubicle/equipment has been earthed where required.

### Additional controls

9. **Removal of Control Fuses**
   - Confirm control fuses have been removed where required.

10. **Padlock(s) and Token(s) Applied**
    - Record that the required personal/system locks and tokens have been applied.

11. **Warning Notice Posted**
    - Confirm the required warning/identification notices have been posted on the relevant equipment/vehicles as specified by the procedure.

12. **Lock-Off Extinguishant System**
    - Confirm the relevant extinguishant/fire-protection system has been isolated or locked off where required.

13. **Heater Isolation**
    - Confirm electrical heaters associated with the equipment have been isolated where required.

14. **Electrical Equipment Disconnected**
    - Confirm the electrical equipment has been physically disconnected where required.

15. **Additional Earthing**
    - Record any additional earthing required by the isolation plan.

## 5.2 Recommended Digital Structure

Do **not** create 15 permanent Boolean fields such as:

```text
breaker_switched_off
racking_out
shutters_locked
...
```

A more maintainable enterprise design is:

```text
ElectricalIsolationRequirement
--------------------------------
electrical_isolation
requirement_type
required
required_status
applied
applied_at
applied_by
remarks
sequence
```

Then the controlled master table can contain the checklist items.

This allows the refinery to add or change isolation controls without a database migration.

# 6. Required vs Applied — Important Business Rule

The distinction between **Required** and **Applied** is critical.

### Required

Answers:

> "Is this isolation control required for this particular isolation?"

Possible values:

- Yes
- No

### Applied

Answers:

> "Has the required control actually been implemented?"

Possible values:

- Yes
- Not yet / incomplete

### Recommended validation

For a release/authorization decision:

```text
If required = Yes
    applied must be Yes
```

A digital system should prevent the isolation from reaching the authorization stage while a required safety control remains unapplied.

This is substantially safer than representing the checklist as a collection of simple unchecked checkboxes.

- when;
- based on which isolation checklist state;

# 17. De-Isolation Should Be a Controlled Process

A professional digital system should not treat de-isolation as:

```python
is_deisolated = True
```

Instead, de-isolation should be a controlled transaction/event.

Before de-isolation is permitted, the system should evaluate the applicable conditions, for example:

```text
Work completed
AND
Cable reconnection completed where required
AND
Personnel clear
AND
Tools/materials removed
AND
Work area released
AND
Required approvals obtained
AND
Required de-isolation authority available
```

The exact conditions must be configurable according to the refinery's approved electrical safety procedure.

# 21. Recommended Status Model

Avoid having many unrelated Boolean fields such as:

```text

isolation_authorized
work_completed
deisolated
closed
cancelled
```

These can create contradictory states.

Instead, use the existing Permit workflow architecture for the overall permit lifecycle and use a dedicated electrical-isolation state machine where necessary.

Example:

```text
DRAFT
REQUESTED
ISOLATION_IN_PROGRESS
ISOLATED
ISOLATION_AUTHORIZED
WORK_IN_PROGRESS
WORK_COMPLETED
DEISOLATION_REQUESTED
DEISOLATED
CLOSED
CANCELLED
```

The actual states should be aligned with your approved Permit Workflow rather than hard-coded if your workflow engine is already configurable.

Electrical isolation is safety-critical. Every significant action should be auditable.

At minimum, record:

```text
who
what
when

new state
reason/comment

```

Important audit events include:

- isolation requested;
- isolation requirement changed;
- isolation control marked applied;
- lock applied;
- lock removed;
- earthing applied;
- earthing removed;
- cable disconnected;
- cable connected;
- isolation authorized;
- work completed;
- cancellation;
- de-isolation requested;
- de-isolation authorized;
- de-isolation performed;
- isolation closed.

A simple `modified_at` field is not sufficient for this level of traceability.

# 28. Key Safety and Data-Integrity Rules

The implementation should enforce rules such as:

### Rule 1 — Equipment must be identifiable

An electrical isolation cannot be created without a valid equipment/tag reference.

### Rule 2 — Required controls must be applied

```text
Required = Yes
        ↓
Applied = Yes
```

must be satisfied before isolation authorization.

### Rule 3 — Authorization requires an authorized role

Only an appropriately authorized electrical isolation role can confirm the isolation.

### Rule 4 — De-isolation is separate from work completion

Completing the work does not automatically remove the electrical isolation.

### Rule 5 — Locks and earthing require traceability

Where the site procedure requires it, lock/earth identifiers and application/removal times should be retained.

### Rule 6 — No destructive deletion

Safety-critical isolation records should normally be retained for audit rather than physically deleted.

### Rule 7 — State changes should be workflow events

Critical transitions should record:

```text
actor
timestamp
role
action
old state
new state
comment
```

### Rule 8 — Historical records must remain immutable

After an isolation is authorized, changing the historical checklist should either be prohibited or create a controlled revision/audit event.

# 29. Important Items Requiring Confirmation From the Refinery Procedure

The paper form alone does not define every technical business rule. Before implementing these as hard-coded choices, confirm:

1. Exact definition of **OWN Isolation**.
2. Official HV/LV voltage classification limits used by the site.
3. Which isolation checklist items are mandatory for each isolation type.
4. Whether absence-of-voltage testing requires a separate test record.
5. Whether locks/tokens have unique serial numbers.
6. Whether every isolation requires earthing.
7. Rules for additional earthing.
8. Who is authorized to apply isolation.
9. Who is authorized to authorize isolation.
10. Who may request de-isolation.
11. Who may remove locks.
12. Whether cable disconnection requires a separate electrical work authorization.
13. Required sequence between cable reconnection, earth removal, lock removal, and energization.
14. Conditions that must be satisfied before de-isolation.
15. Whether the electrical isolation record has to remain attached to the Permit after closure.
16. Required retention period for the isolation/audit record.

These should come from the refinery's approved electrical safety/isolation procedure rather than being inferred from the form.

## Summary

The most important design decision is to treat this document as a **structured electrical isolation transaction associated with a Permit-to-Work**, not as a static form.

The paper form's strongest concepts to preserve are:

- equipment/tag identification;
- isolation classification;
- HV/LV classification;
- **Required vs Applied** isolation controls;
- earthing traceability;
- padlock/token traceability;
- electrical specialist authorization;
- cable disconnection/reconnection;
- U/V/W terminal identification;
- work completion/cancellation;
- controlled de-isolation;
- named responsible persons;
- date/time/signoff;
- complete audit history.
